# Mission 001 — Decision Pass 001: Pantry Launch Direction

**Date:** 2026-09-30
**Status:** EVIDENCE-BACKED OPERATING DIRECTION; CAPEX/UNIT ECONOMICS STILL REQUIRE FIELD QUOTES
**Scope:** Senegal first. Khoudia pantry system.

## Decision correction

Mission 001 does NOT select rice merely because Senegal imports large quantities. Rice and Mission 002 oilseed/nut candidates must be compared on the same commercial basis.

Current operating direction:
1. Start Khoudia market validation with Senegalese rice as the mass-frequency anchor.
2. Develop groundnut paste/noflaye as the first groundnut transformation to validate in parallel; do not begin with a large oil-crushing/refining plant.
3. Keep groundnut oil as a later/parallel product contingent on procurement, aflatoxin, refining/filtration, competitive vegetable-oil pricing and utilization economics.
4. Treat sesame as a value-add/export/ingredient candidate, not a first mass-volume anchor.
5. Open cashew as a dedicated value-leakage investigation because reported exports are large but ANSD flags unreliable national production data.
6. Keep balanites/soump, moringa, baobab and other discovered seeds/nuts in specialty discovery; do not force them into the first pantry launch.

## Evidence snapshot

### Rice
- Senegal produced 946,209 t rice in 2024/25 (ANSD/Direction de l'Agriculture).
- Senegal imported 1,387,343 t rice in 2024; import value was 315.039bn CFA.
- 2025 rice import value increased to 336.734bn CFA; ANSD unit value 228 CFA/kg.
- Dec 2025 Dakar retail: local ordinary broken rice 383 CFA/kg; imported fragrant broken rice 499 CFA/kg.
- AfricaRice: Sahel varieties dominate >90% irrigated rice area in Senegal River Valley; farmer-field mean 6–7 t/ha. Aromatic Sahel lines and ISRIZ lines provide a quality/premium test path.
- ISRA officially registered ISRIZ 16, 17, P01, P02; AfricaRice lists additional ISRIZ/Sahel varieties by agronomic and grain/cooking traits.
- SISMAR offers Senegal-made rice hullers 250/500 kg/h, rice threshers 1,000–1,500 kg/h and cereal transformation lines 800–1,000 kg/h.

### Groundnut
- Senegal produced 795,585 t in 2024/25; final 2025/26 ANSD bulletin records a larger campaign (retain exact current table evidence in claim ledger).
- Official producer floor is 305 CFA/kg for 2025/26, but APS field reporting found 225–275 and 250–300 CFA/kg in some markets; shelled kernels 600–700 CFA/kg in Tivaouane reporting.
- Jan 2025 Dakar ANSD retail: peanut paste 1,091 CFA/kg; groundnut oil 1,818 CFA/L; generic vegetable oil 1,463 CFA/L.
- 2024 Senegal groundnut-oil exports: about 6.086m kg / US$11.457m (WITS/UN Comtrade).
- Recent value-chain research estimates SONACOS utilization at only 35–40%, with procurement/raw-material competition, seed quality, finance and narrow product portfolio among constraints.
- ANSD agricultural microdata explicitly records household transformation into noflaye, peanut paste, groundnut oil and cake.
- SISMAR offers groundnut shellers 400–600 kg/h, paste grinders 150–200 kg/h and complete groundnut units including storage, elevators, presses and roasters.
- Aflatoxin is a mandatory economic/safety gate; ITA has a mycotoxin laboratory and work on reducing aflatoxin in crude artisanal groundnut oil.

### Sesame
- 2024/25 production 18,573 t.
- 2024 reported sesame-seed exports 27.066m kg / US$20.052m, mostly China. Export volume exceeding the same-campaign production estimate is a reconciliation flag: timing, stocks, re-export/cross-border flows and reporting must be resolved.
- Candidate transformations: cleaned/graded seed, hulled seed, toasted ingredient, paste, culinary oil, cosmetic ingredient, meal/cake subject to specifications.
- Do not assign mass-market pantry priority until local demand, buyer specs, yields and margins are evidenced.

### Cashew
- WITS/Comtrade reports Senegal 2024 cashew exports of 79.129m kg / US$86.380m, mainly India and Vietnam.
- ANSD explicitly says reported export volumes were unusually high relative to weak national production data and commissioned EMFAS to quantify actors, production, trade and value added.
- Therefore cashew processing leakage is a strong hypothesis, but Senegal-grown feedstock availability remains unresolved.
- Candidate products: kernels, roasted/seasoned kernels, pieces/ingredients, cashew butter/paste, apple products; shell streams require dedicated safety/industrial analysis.

### Other seeds/nuts
- ANSD agricultural collection categories include soump/balanites, shea, moringa and baobab-related products.
- Balanites processing is already demonstrated in Senegal with dehulling/milling/steaming/oil pressing and local/urban sale.
- These remain specialty candidates until sustainable supply, food safety, yield, repeat demand and unit economics are established.

## Product tree rule

Every crop is researched as a material tree, not one SKU.

Rice: premium/ordinary/broken grades -> flour/ingredients where justified -> bran -> husk -> rejects.
Groundnut: edible kernels -> roasted/seasoned -> paste/noflaye/butter -> oil -> cake/meal -> shells -> rejects.
Sesame: cleaned seed -> hulled/toasted -> paste -> oil -> meal/cake -> hulls/rejects.
Cashew: kernels/grades/pieces -> roasted/flavored -> paste/butter -> apple streams -> shell streams only under safe industrial handling.
No co-product receives revenue without an evidenced buyer, specification and safe disposition.

## First operating sequence

### Track A — Khoudia rice pilot
Do not build a large mill first.
1. Select 2–4 locally appropriate rice varieties/grades for cooking and milling trials.
2. Source from farmer/cooperative partners using documented lot specifications.
3. Test moisture, foreign matter, varietal purity, milling recovery, broken fraction, cooking performance and blind consumer preference.
4. Use qualified existing/contract milling for the first commercial validation unless owned equipment has a proven cost/quality advantage.
5. Own/control QC, lot traceability, packaging specification, brand, merchant relationships and customer data from day one.
6. Test pack sizes from evidence; 5 kg is not assumed.
7. Test ordinary-value and better-cooking/aromatic propositions before locking one.
8. Scale only after repeat purchase, procurement reliability, quality pass rate, merchant reorder and cash-cycle gates are met.

### Track B — Groundnut pantry pilot
Do not start with a large crusher.
1. Procure food-grade lots with aflatoxin sampling/segregation.
2. Validate clean kernels and groundnut paste/noflaye first because the process is simpler and the category is already observed in ANSD price/household transformation data.
3. Test roasted/seasoned kernels where consumer/channel evidence supports them.
4. Run oil pressing trials separately; compare true oil + cake economics against generic vegetable-oil competition.
5. Do not value cake until feed-mill specifications, aflatoxin limits, delivered prices and substitution constraints are known.

## Seed/farmer program

Rice trials should include appropriate Sahel/ISRIZ candidates selected for ecology plus grain/cooking/milling quality, not yield alone.
Groundnut seed work must address the recent finding that poor access to quality seed is a major farmer constraint.
For both systems: certified/verified seed source, farmer training, harvest timing, drying, moisture, impurity control, storage, lot identity and quality-linked purchasing incentives.

## Machinery strategy

Order of preference:
1. existing Senegalese machine/capacity;
2. shared/contract capacity;
3. inspected used equipment (including Chinese secondary marketplaces such as Xianyu/Goofish when suitable);
4. new imported component/machine only when justified;
5. progressively redesign/fabricate locally.

Every acquired machine becomes a learning asset: record BOM, motor, bearings, screens, shafts, controls, tolerances, throughput, yield/loss, energy, sanitation, downtime, wear parts and failure modes.

Do not publish a factory CAPEX until written quotes are collected for Senegal-made equipment and comparable used/imported configurations. Public SISMAR pages provide throughput/configuration but not reliable purchase prices.

## Immediate field/quote closure tasks

1. Written SISMAR quote: rice huller 250/500 kg/h; cereal line 800–1,000 kg/h; rice thresher 1–1.5 t/h.
2. Written SISMAR quote: groundnut sheller 400–600 kg/h; paste grinder 150–200 kg/h; roaster; press; complete modular unit.
3. Three existing rice mill quotes: tolling fee/kg, minimum lot, recovery, quality controls, downtime and ownership of bran/husk/broken fractions.
4. Groundnut processor quotes: shelling, roasting, grinding and pressing yields/costs.
5. Packaging quotes for likely pilot sizes and food-contact barrier requirements.
6. ITA/qualified lab quote for rice quality and groundnut aflatoxin testing.
7. Merchant interviews: retail/wholesale price, margin, case size, credit, reorder and preferred pack sizes.
8. Household/foodservice blind tests for rice cooking and groundnut paste.
9. Farmer/cooperative quotes by crop/grade/season and payment terms.
10. Used-equipment inspection quotes and landed-cost comparisons; no marketplace sticker price is accepted as installed cost.

## Product #1 decision rule

Current evidence supports **rice as the mass-frequency pilot and groundnut paste/noflaye as the strongest first parallel transformation to test**, but the final commercial lock remains conditional on the field/quote tasks above.

The final lock must be based on comparable:
raw material cost; usable yield; process cost; packaging; labor; logistics; merchant margin; marketing; working capital; safety; shelf life; repeat demand; gross contribution; farmer impact; jobs/capability; co-product disposition; machinery localization; and scale failure conditions.

## Mission closure rule

Mission 001 is not fully CLOSED until real supplier/mill/lab/merchant/farmer quotes replace decisive UNKNOWNs. It is sufficiently resolved to begin controlled pilots without waiting for a large owned factory.
