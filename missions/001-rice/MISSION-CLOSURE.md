# Mission 001 — Pantry Staple Decision & Rice System Closure

**Mission:** mission-001  
**State:** REOPENED FOR COMPLETE PRODUCT DECISION  
**Rule:** Mission 001 does not presume rice is Khoudia Product #1.

## Decision question

What should Khoudia's first pantry product be, and if rice survives the comparison, exactly how should the first Senegal rice business be sourced, processed, packaged, marketed, distributed and scaled?

Mission 001 must settle the rice case completely enough to compare it fairly with Mission 002 nuts/oilseeds. No product is selected because it was the founder's first idea.

## Khoudia goal

Build pantry products people already want to buy repeatedly: excellent everyday food, African-produced and increasingly African-processed, professionally branded, widely distributed, affordable for the intended customer, commercially durable, and capable of creating productive jobs and supplier capability.

"Local" is not the product proposition by itself. Taste, cooking performance, cleanliness, consistency, convenience, price/value, availability and trust must win.

## Rice questions that MUST be closed

### 1. Demand and customer
- Current Senegal rice consumption and import/local supply using current 2024–2026 evidence.
- Household vs restaurant/foodservice demand.
- Broken/whole grain, aromatic/non-aromatic, parboiled/non-parboiled and other material segments.
- Actual dishes/use cases, cooking behavior and purchase frequency.
- Who chooses/pays, where they shop, current brands/unbranded alternatives, complaints and switching reasons.
- Actual pack sizes and prices by channel; do not assume 5 kg.
- Blind cooking/product tests and repeat-purchase evidence before scale.

### 2. Seed and farm system
- Identify Senegal-appropriate varieties and seed sources; distinguish certified seed, research material and commercial availability.
- Compare yield, maturity, ecology, irrigation/rainfed fit, disease/pest resilience, grain quality, milling recovery, breakage, cooking/taste and farmer economics.
- Research improved Sahel/ISRIZ/NERICA and other relevant varieties without selecting by name alone.
- Farmer/cooperative sourcing geographies, production calendar, farmgate economics, input/finance constraints and quality incentives.
- Define what Khoudia can improve upstream without making unsupported yield promises.

### 3. Post-harvest specification
- Harvest/threshing practices, drying method, target moisture evidence, foreign matter, stones, varietal mixing, storage, pests and losses.
- Cleaning, destoning, grading and lot segregation.
- Traceability from farmer/cooperative through finished batch.
- Quality acceptance/rejection rules and economic incentive for better lots.

### 4. Milling and process train
- Map existing usable milling capacity and actual utilization before proposing new capacity.
- Compare contract milling, leased/shared capacity, used equipment, Senegal-made equipment and owned mill.
- Establish mass balance per 1,000 kg paddy: finished rice grades, broken fractions, bran, husk, rejects, moisture/process loss.
- Every co-product needs a safe disposition and evidenced buyer/specification before receiving economic value.
- Compare minimum viable industrial, locally optimized scale-up and larger plant only after utilization/demand triggers.

### 5. Machinery and African capability
For cleaner, destoner, grader, dryer, huller/dehusker, polisher if justified, sorter, conveyors, scales and packer:
- required function/tolerance/throughput;
- Senegal/African supplier or fabricator;
- used-equipment option including Chinese secondary markets such as Xianyu/Goofish where appropriate;
- local BOM and components;
- fabricate-local-now / local-after-training / import-required;
- motors, bearings, screens, shafts, controls, sensors and wear parts;
- power, maintenance, sanitation, safety and spares;
- purchase/inspection/freight/duty/installation cost evidence.
Use existing equipment first when sensible, learn from operation, improve/localize progressively, and avoid turnkey Western-cost assumptions.

### 6. Packaging and product
- Determine exact first rice specification from evidence, not branding preference.
- Test package sizes/materials, resealability where valuable, shelf life, transport abuse, stacking and merchant display.
- Khoudia packaging may use the giant-letter/window concept only after food-protection/manufacturability testing.
- Required label languages, origin, nutrition, lot/traceability and truthful certifications.
- QR provenance/recipes can be tested as media, not assumed to drive purchase.

### 7. Route to market and marketing
Map farmer/co-op -> processor -> warehouse -> distributor -> wholesaler -> market/boutique/stall -> restaurant/consumer.
Research merchant margin, commission, MOQ, case size, credit, reorder interval, delivery cost, stockout/returns, route density and rep productivity.
Marketing must identify actual chooser/payer and test sampling, cooks/creators, recipes, neighborhood merchant activation, social/video and only then TV/radio if incremental economics justify it.
Country expansion must be researched independently; continental ambition does not imply identical products/packaging.

### 8. Bottom-up economics
Build per-kg and per-pack economics from farmgate/paddy through consumer:
raw material; collection; drying; storage; milling; loss; QC/testing; packaging; labor; utilities; maintenance; depreciation; logistics; distributor/merchant economics; marketing; working capital; taxes/duties where applicable; cash buffer.
Separate CAPEX from working capital.
Unknown costs remain UNKNOWN and trigger quote/field tasks.
Model price/yield/utilization sensitivity and procurement seasonality.

### 9. Labor and capability
Headcount by function and shift: procurement/field, quality, operators, maintenance/fabrication, packing, warehouse, drivers/logistics, sales, finance/admin, marketing.
Evidence wages/benefits/productivity where possible.
Specify training and advancement pathways and Maagal-relevant capability gaps.

### 10. Launch plan
If rice survives:
- exact first SKU/customer/geography;
- first farmer/cooperative sourcing area and sourcing radius;
- first production volume;
- contract/shared/owned processing choice;
- equipment owned on day 1;
- QC specification;
- packaging supplier/configuration;
- first merchants/restaurants;
- 90-day operating ramp;
- 12/24/36-month expansion triggers;
- stop/kill conditions.

## Fair first-product comparison

Mission 001 cannot declare Product #1 until its rice economics can be compared on the same basis with Mission 002 candidates including groundnut kernels/snacks, groundnut paste/butter, groundnut oil, sesame products, cashew products and independently discovered pantry candidates.

Comparison inputs must include raw-material availability/cost, processing yield, equipment/capex, working capital, safety, shelf life, packaging, labor, gross contribution, repeat demand, market size, competition, distribution complexity, merchant economics, farmer value, jobs/capabilities, co-products, local-machinery potential, Africa expansion and failure conditions.

No weighted score or founder preference substitutes for the underlying evidence. The final human decision receives comparable facts and explicit uncertainties.

## Definition of DONE

Mission 001 is complete only when it can produce an evidence-backed build case:

**Start [specific product] in [specific geography], source [specific input/specification] from [sourcing system], process at [throughput] using [contract/shared/owned train], own/fabricate [equipment], employ [headcount/capabilities], require [CAPEX + working capital], sell through [channels with merchant economics], launch with [tested marketing], use/dispose co-products as [evidenced], and scale only when [predefined triggers].**

If any decisive input is unknown, Mission 001 remains open.
