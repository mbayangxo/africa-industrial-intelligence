# Mission 002 — Buildability, cost and execution research specification

**Research date:** 2026-09-30  
**Status:** ACTIVE  
**Founder requirement:** research must be deep enough to decide what to build, how to start it, what it costs under Senegalese/African conditions, who works in it, how it reaches customers, and how it scales.

## A. Rolling evidence window — mandatory

Never base a current build decision on one calendar year. Maintain:
- 2024 actuals;
- 2025 actuals/final publications;
- 2025/26 agricultural/commercialization campaign;
- latest available 2026 monthly/quarterly/campaign evidence;
- earlier history when needed to identify structural vs temporary conditions.

For every number record calendar year vs marketing/crop year, provisional/final status, methodology, geography, unit and source date.

### Current examples that prove why
- ANSD final 2025 trade analysis: unroasted-groundnut export receipts fell to 1.4bn CFA in 2025 from 65.3bn CFA in 2024.
- 2025/26 commercialization: official producer price 305 CFA/kg; export-tax suspension and an announced 300k–450k t export window altered incentives.
- Diourbel SONACOS collection for 2025/26 reported 38,247.937 t, versus about 22,000 t in 2025 and 2,000 t in 2024 at that facility/area reporting context; collection cost reported at 16.825bn CFA. Do not generalize one regional plant to national performance.
- Government/SONACOS reporting also identifies financing, collection-point functionality and heavy-equipment availability as live constraints.
- ANSD August 2026 CPI: overall consumer prices +1.2% y/y; local-origin products +1.8%, imported-origin products -0.6%. Product-specific oil prices still require separate evidence.

## B. Ingenuity-first cost engineering

For each candidate business, build three engineering/cost configurations rather than importing a Western turnkey quote:

### Configuration 1 — minimum viable industrial
Smallest safe/compliant setup capable of proving product-market fit and stable quality.

### Configuration 2 — locally optimized scale-up
Reuse/modify existing machines; Senegal/local fabrication; local assembly using standard motors/bearings/steel/tanks/frames; shared laboratory/warehouse/utilities where rational; import only precision or irreducible components.

### Configuration 3 — larger industrial
Only after utilization, procurement and demand justify capacity.

For every process step research:
- required function and quality tolerance;
- throughput range;
- manual/semi-auto/automatic options;
- existing Senegalese machinery/fabricators;
- components/materials/BOM;
- what can be made locally now;
- what could be made locally after tooling/training;
- irreducible imports;
- supplier quotations or local market checks;
- freight/duty/installation where applicable;
- electricity/fuel/water;
- operators per shift;
- maintenance labor;
- consumables/spares;
- expected downtime;
- cleaning/sanitation;
- food/feed safety controls;
- building/civil works;
- lifecycle cost and replacement cycle.

Do not use generic Western plant prices as the baseline. If no Senegal price exists, label estimate and derive bottom-up from components, labor, material, fabrication and importable subassemblies. Obtain field quotes before investment.

## C. Business-start architecture

Every promoted candidate must include:
1. exact first product/service and customer;
2. first geography and sourcing radius;
3. minimum viable throughput;
4. site/building requirements;
5. equipment train;
6. supplier/procurement contracts;
7. licenses/standards/testing;
8. staffing and training;
9. packaging;
10. working capital and inventory cycle;
11. route to market;
12. merchant/distributor economics;
13. marketing launch;
14. 90-day commissioning/ramp;
15. 12/24/36-month expansion triggers;
16. downside/stop rules.

Separate:
- one-time capex;
- pre-opening;
- initial raw material;
- packaging inventory;
- payroll;
- utilities;
- logistics;
- marketing;
- distributor/merchant credit;
- contingency;
- cash buffer.

## D. Labor and jobs

Research Senegalese wages and actual local hiring markets by role; statutory minimums are floors, not wage assumptions.

Build staffing by process and shift:
- plant manager;
- procurement/field coordinators;
- quality/food-safety lead;
- laboratory/testing;
- machine operators;
- maintenance/fabrication technicians;
- sort/clean/pack labor;
- warehouse;
- drivers/logistics;
- sales/merchant reps;
- finance/admin;
- marketing/content/community;
- seasonal labor.

For each: headcount, skill, training path, wage/benefits evidence, shift coverage, productivity target, advancement path and which skills can be taught through Maagal Cayor/future technical training rather than imported expertise.

Track jobs created AND productive capability created.

## E. Marketing research — from the beginning

Marketing is not a launch afterthought or generic ad budget.

Research:
- who chooses and pays for each product;
- household/foodservice/industrial segments;
- Wolof/Pulaar/French and other relevant language use;
- dishes/use cases;
- trusted cooks, food creators, merchants and community figures;
- sampling/tasting and blind-comparison design;
- social/video behavior;
- TV/radio only where incremental economics justify it;
- package as media: QR provenance, recipes, batch traceability, story;
- repeat-purchase measurement;
- acquisition cost and creator cost through real quotes;
- neighborhood merchant activation;
- country-specific creative for expansion.

Founder-originated concept to test, not assume: recurring content featuring women named Khoudia cooking local dishes; test recognition, trust, memorability and conversion against alternatives.

2025 digital context: DataReportal estimates 11.3m internet users and 5.01m social-media user identities in Senegal at start-2025; treat ad-reach metrics as planning signals, not unique-user counts.

## F. Distribution and merchant economics

Map formal + informal:
producer/co-op → processor → warehouse → distributor → wholesaler → market/boutique/stall → foodservice/consumer.

Research:
- retailer/distributor gross margin;
- commissions/incentives;
- minimum order;
- case size;
- payment terms/credit;
- reorder frequency;
- delivery frequency;
- stockouts;
- returns/damage;
- route density;
- sales-rep productivity;
- neighborhood storage;
- regional stock points;
- transport cost per case/kg/litre;
- digital reorder/payment opportunity through Jokko later.

Merchant economic participation/ownership is a design hypothesis; do not promise equity casually. Compare margin, commission, volume rebate, cooperative participation and legally coherent ownership structures.

## G. Continental + diaspora architecture

Design reusable systems from day one:
traceability, barcode, quality records, modular labels/languages, case/pallet specs, distributor master data, logistics interfaces.

But research each market independently:
consumer, cuisine, language, pack size, purchasing power, regulation, retail structure, creators/media and pricing.

Investigate African stores/wholesalers and foodservice in diaspora markets only after product/export compliance and landed economics are known.

## H. Whole-feedstock economics

For each nut/seed create a per-1,000-kg feedstock mass-and-value balance:
- purchased feedstock;
- cleaning loss;
- shell/hull;
- edible kernels;
- oil;
- cake/meal;
- fines/rejects;
- moisture change;
- process loss;
- packaging;
- energy;
- labor;
- logistics;
- selling price by stream;
- buyer specification;
- safety disposition;
- gross contribution by stream.

No by-product receives value unless an evidenced buyer/specification/use exists.

## I. Business candidates requiring bottom-up startup models

1. Collection-point cleaning/sorting equipment + service.
2. Groundnut quality/procurement/storage network.
3. Small whole-groundnut food + oil processor.
4. Aflatoxin prevention/testing/segregation service.
5. Feed-ingredient/formulation business using safe co-products.
6. Sesame cleaning/grading/hulling.
7. Sesame paste/premium oil where evidence supports.
8. Regional crude-oil refining/packaging.
9. Local oilseed machinery + maintenance company.
10. Shared processing service/cooperative infrastructure.

The research agents may add candidates; they must also reject weak ones.

## J. Output standard

A candidate is not ready because a report says "large market." A decision packet must eventually be able to state:

> Start with [business/product] in [place], sourcing [input] through [model], at [throughput], using [equipment train], of which [components] are locally fabricated/modified, employing [roles/headcount], requiring [evidence-backed capex + working capital range], selling through [channels] at [merchant economics], launched through [tested marketing], with [co-product uses], and scale only when [measurable triggers] are reached.

Until those inputs are evidenced, the system must say UNKNOWN rather than manufacture a budget.
