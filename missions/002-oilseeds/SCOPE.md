# Mission 002 — Senegal nuts, oilseeds, oils and co-products

**Plan status:** LAUNCHED — DESK RESEARCH AUTHORIZED  
**Research status:** ACTIVE — EVIDENCE ACQUISITION  
**Launch authority:** Founder  
**Decision order:** compare feedstocks → map complete value chains → discover opportunities → red team → build decisions

## Mission question

Which nut/oilseed value chains can create the strongest combination of competitive food/products, import substitution or exports, farmer and industrial value addition, useful co-products, locally buildable processing capability, and scalable African distribution — and which should we not build?

Groundnut/peanut is a major hypothesis, not the predetermined winner.

## Comparison set

### Core comparison
- Groundnut / peanut
- Sesame

### Required comparator screen
Screen other technically or commercially relevant oil-bearing crops/products evidenced in Senegal or realistic regional sourcing, including soybean, sunflower, palm/palm kernel, shea, baobab seed, moringa seed and other candidates discovered independently. A comparator enters deep research only when evidence supports supply, demand, industrial relevance or a meaningful differentiated market.

Food, feed, cosmetic and industrial grades are separate markets and must not be blended into one opportunity score.

## Compare every candidate on the same dimensions

1. Senegal/regional feedstock availability, seasonality, yields and competing uses.
2. Farmgate/aggregation economics and producer constraints.
3. Domestic consumption and buyer behavior.
4. Current imports/exports and realistically contestable value.
5. Product tree: whole/snack/paste/butter/flour/protein/oil and other evidenced products.
6. Oil pathway: extraction yield, crude/refined requirements, filtration/refining, quality, shelf life and packaging.
7. Co-products/material streams: cake/meal, shells/hulls, fines and other streams; composition, safety, buyer specifications and economics.
8. Food/feed safety: aflatoxin where relevant, contaminants, moisture/storage, testing, detoxification limits, standards and traceability.
9. Machinery and ingenuity: reuse/modify → local fabrication → local assembly → shared service → irreducible imported components → complete imported equipment only when justified.
10. Utilities, storage, laboratories, maintenance, spares, logistics and working capital.
11. Route to market: wholesalers, markets, boutiques/stalls, foodservice, industrial buyers, distributors, merchant economics and reorder behavior.
12. Consumer/product quality and marketing.
13. Continental African and diaspora expansion potential, researched market by market.
14. Competitive intensity, substitutes and incumbent response.
15. Capital intensity, operating economics, utilization risk and scale pathways.
16. Skills, productive assets, supplier capability and locally retained value.
17. Environmental/resource constraints and circularity.
18. Fatal risks, reasons not to build and evidence needed to falsify the thesis.

## Whole-feedstock rule

Never evaluate an oil plant on oil revenue alone. Build a mass/material balance for the complete feedstock and investigate every material stream before calling it waste. Oil, edible kernels/products, cake/meal, shells/hulls and other outputs must each have evidenced disposition, safety constraints, buyer specifications and logistics.

## Independence rule

Run one discovery packet blinded from founder-proposed products and brands. At least half of independent-cycle candidates must not directly derive from founder ideas. The comparison may therefore discover a better business than packaged cooking oil.

## Initial evidence signal — not a conclusion

Current public evidence already indicates that groundnut quality/cleaning remains an active policy and industrial issue: Senegal's agriculture ministry says its 2026–2030 mechanization program plans cleaning/sorting equipment across collection points, with equipment already being deployed for the 2025–2026 campaign. This is a research lead, not proof of a private-sector opportunity.

Trade data also show substantial 2024 imports of soybean oil-cake/meal into Senegal. That makes animal-feed/protein inputs a required adjacency to investigate when comparing local press-cake opportunities, but it does not establish that groundnut cake can technically or economically substitute for soybean meal.

## Immediate acquisition queue

1. Current official Senegal production/area/yield series by candidate crop and region.
2. Current trade by HS code for seeds/nuts, crude/refined oils, prepared nut products and meals/cakes.
3. Processor/crusher/refiner capacity and utilization evidence.
4. Groundnut quality, cleaning, storage and aflatoxin evidence.
5. Consumer cooking-oil and nut-product assortment/pricing evidence.
6. Feed-industry demand and nutritional/specification evidence for cakes/meals.
7. Process yields and mass balances from authoritative technical sources.
8. Machinery functions and locally fabricable component map.
9. Packaging, shelf-life, distribution and merchant economics.
10. Independent discovery search packet.

## Promotion rule

No commodity, product or factory becomes a recommendation because it ranks well on one dimension. Promotion requires traceable evidence, comparable definitions, safety/standards review, complete material balance, route-to-market evidence, ingenuity review, economics/scenarios when eligible, and independent Skeptic review.
