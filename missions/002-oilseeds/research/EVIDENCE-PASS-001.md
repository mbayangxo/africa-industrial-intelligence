# Mission 002 — Evidence pass 001

**Research date:** 2026-09-30  
**Status:** preliminary evidence acquisition; no build recommendation

## Comparable production baseline

- ANSD / Direction de l’Agriculture reports 2024–2025 Senegal production of groundnuts at 795,585 tonnes and sesame at 18,573 tonnes. Groundnuts were 1,057,836 tonnes and sesame 21,759 tonnes in 2023–2024. These figures establish scale and year-to-year variability; they do not establish available industrial surplus or processor economics.
  - Source: ANSD, *Situation Economique et Sociale du Sénégal Ed. 2024 — Agriculture*, Table XIX.4.

## Trade signals

- WITS/UN Comtrade reports Senegal exported 27,066,300 kg of sesame seed worth USD 20.052 million in 2024. China accounted for 23,494,800 kg in the reporter-side series. This is evidence of a raw/less-processed export stream worth investigating for value addition; it is not proof that domestic sesame-oil processing is superior to seed export.
- WITS reports Senegal exported 6,085,690 kg of groundnut oil/fractions worth USD 11.457 million in 2024. This establishes an existing oil export pathway.
- WITS reports 2024 Senegal soybean-oil imports of USD 11.194 million.
- WITS reports 2024 imports in HS 1512 (sunflower/safflower/cottonseed oils and fractions) of USD 22.558 million. The aggregate HS heading must not be described as sunflower alone.
- WITS reports 2024 soybean meal/oil-cake imports of 138,211,000 kg worth USD 63.143 million. This is a major protein/feed import signal, but substitution by local groundnut/sesame cake is a hypothesis requiring nutritional, safety, formulation, price and supply evidence.
- WITS also reports imports of other vegetable-oil cakes/residues (HS 2306). Reporter and partner-side datasets show discrepancies in totals; preserve the disagreement and reconcile before quantitative use.

## Early hypotheses to test — not conclusions

1. **Groundnut whole-system processing:** Senegal's large groundnut base may support stronger economics when edible products, oil, cake/meal and shells are optimized together rather than evaluating bottled oil alone.
2. **Sesame value-add leakage:** Senegal exports substantial sesame seed while domestic sesame-oil imports are tiny. Test whether higher-value cleaning/grading, hulled sesame, tahini/paste, ingredients or oil beat raw-seed export after yield, quality, buyer and capital economics.
3. **Feed-protein adjacency:** The scale of imported soybean meal makes animal-feed protein a required comparison. Test actual amino-acid/protein specifications, aflatoxin constraints, livestock segments, formulation inclusion limits and delivered cost before claiming substitution.
4. **Imported edible-oil basket:** Soybean and HS-1512 oil imports indicate demand beyond groundnut oil. Investigate whether Senegal should grow/process alternative oilseeds, refine regionally sourced crude oils, improve groundnut competitiveness, or combine pathways.
5. **Processing machinery opportunity:** cleaning, sorting, shelling/dehulling, drying, pressing, filtration, refining, cake handling, testing and packaging machinery must each pass the ingenuity hierarchy before turnkey imports are considered.

## Data-quality warning

Mirror trade statistics disagree materially for some Senegal flows. Never combine reporter-side and partner-side values as though they are one consistent series. Record reporter, partner, HS revision, year, quantity unit and source side, then reconcile.
