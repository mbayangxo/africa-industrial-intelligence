# Mission 002 — Deep evidence pass 002: industrial system

**Research date:** 2026-09-30  
**Status:** ACTIVE RESEARCH — no recommendation  
**Purpose:** move beyond trade totals into procurement, processing, safety, co-products, machinery, consumer market and capital-allocation questions.

## 1. Production baseline and methodology warning

ANSD/Direction de l'Agriculture reports 2024/25 production of 795,585 t groundnuts from 780,072 ha (reported yield 1,020 kg/ha) and 18,573 t sesame from 24,916 ha (745 kg/ha). ANSD's April 2026 bulletin reports provisional/current campaign totals of 940,198 t groundnuts from 809,672 ha (1,161 kg/ha) and 29,482 t sesame from 38,389 ha (768 kg/ha).

USDA's MY series differs (731,000 t estimate for 2024/25; 770,000 t forecast for 2025/26) and explicitly says Senegal changed crop-production methodology from MY 2024/25. Preserve the series separately; do not splice them.

Sources: ANSD SESN 2024 agriculture; ANSD April 2026 bulletin; USDA FAS 2025 Senegal Peanut Annual.

## 2. Procurement may be more binding than installed crushing capacity

USDA reported that by 24 April 2025 SONACOS had collected about 155,500 t of peanuts, while COPEOL and SSII had not collected in the campaign because financing negotiations with banks were unresolved. A 2026 groundnut-system study based on 503 farmers in 18 villages plus focus groups/key-informant interviews describes formal processor utilization around 35–40%, high unit costs, competition from traders/export buyers and weak incentives for quality/storage.

**Implication to test:** do not recommend new crushing capacity until procurement finance, raw-material capture, utilization, quality and differentiated product economics are modeled. Existing underutilization could make a new plant value-destructive even when national crop tonnage looks large.

## 3. Cleaning/sorting is a live industrial problem with local manufacturing precedent

The Ministry of Agriculture says PMAS 2026–2030 plans 20,000 screens and 1,500 tarares for 1,200 groundnut collection points. For 2025/26 it announced 1,900 screens and 100 tarares with 40% subsidy. It identifies sand, harvest residue and pod fragments as competitiveness problems. The same source says Senegalese manufacturer SISMAR is producing this equipment.

**Implication to test:** cleaning/sorting equipment and collection-point quality systems are an evidenced machinery market, but government procurement/subsidy may alter private-market economics. Investigate component BOM, throughput, maintenance, price, manufacturing bottlenecks and adjacent crops before proposing entry.

## 4. Safety is not a downstream lab check; it starts at drying/storage

A 2025 Senegal diagnosis links ground-level drying and exposure to rain/humidity to fungal vulnerability and aflatoxin risk. Senegal's ITA mycotoxin laboratory tests aflatoxins and has developed a treatment for reducing aflatoxin in crude artisanal groundnut oil. ASN opened a 2025 national public inquiry on an ECOWAS/Senegal code of practice for prevention/reduction of aflatoxin contamination in peanuts.

**System requirement:** model safety chain as harvest timing → drying → moisture → storage → sorting → sampling/testing → segregation → processing → oil/cake disposition. A contaminated lot cannot be assumed to become safe feed merely because oil is processed.

## 5. Whole-groundnut material balance

Feedipedia reports a Senegal-specific traditional-processing reference: dehulling 100 kg peanut fruits yields 25–35 kg shells; after skin removal/grinding/cooking/pressing, 25–40 kg oil cake is produced. Mechanical cake may retain roughly 5–20% oil, while solvent-extracted meal can be below 1% residual oil.

These are technical reference ranges, not a commercial plant yield model. Mission 002 must acquire cultivar/moisture/process-specific mass balances before economics.

Potential streams requiring separate evidence:
- edible/HPS kernels;
- crude/refined oil;
- press cake/meal;
- shells;
- skins/fines/rejects;
- contaminated fractions and their legally safe disposition.

SONACOS itself markets refined groundnut oil, crude groundnut oil, HPS kernels, peanut cake and shells, confirming that several streams already have commercial pathways in Senegal.

## 6. Feed-protein opportunity: large demand signal, non-equivalent ingredients

2024 Senegal soybean meal/cake imports: 138.211 million kg, USD 63.143 million (WITS/UN Comtrade). Soybean meal is the benchmark plant-protein ingredient; high-protein grades are typically 47–49% protein. Sesame expeller meal is reported around 32–53% protein (about 45% DM typical), but is relatively lysine-poor and richer in sulfur amino acids. Peanut cake composition and aflatoxin risk depend heavily on raw material and extraction.

**Decision rule:** compare least-cost feed formulations by species/stage and digestible amino-acid/nutrient constraints, not price per tonne or crude protein alone. No 1:1 substitution claim.

## 7. Edible-oil import basket is much larger than soy alone

WITS/UN Comtrade 2024 reporter-side signals:
- crude palm oil: USD 40.056m, 107.231m kg;
- non-crude palm oil/liquid fractions: USD 73.489m, 66.479m kg;
- soybean oil/fractions: USD 11.194m;
- non-crude sunflower/safflower oil: USD 15.647m;
- groundnut oil exports: USD 11.457m, 6.086m kg.

Do not add categories into a market-size claim until HS overlap, re-exports, crude-to-refined conversion and domestic refining flows are reconciled.

**Key question:** Senegal may have at least four distinct strategies—domestic oilseed expansion, better groundnut utilization/value-add, regional African crude-oil sourcing/refining, and differentiated premium oils. They require separate economics.

## 8. Consumer/channel evidence

ANSD's Dakar consumer-price bulletin reports vegetable oil around 1,500 CFA/litre in the cited November 2025 table and also tracks 12.5 cl sachets/dosettes, evidence that very small formats matter. Auchan Senegal's online assortment currently shows mass-market 1 L/5 L vegetable/soy/sunflower formats plus a 250 ml sesame oil at a much higher unit price. Retail-web prices are channel observations, not national prices.

SONACOS lists Niani groundnut oil in 1 L, 5 L and 15 L formats and Niinal vegetable oil in the same formats, plus 20 L professional/industrial products.

**Fieldwork requirement:** quantify actual channel share, refill/bulk behavior, sachet use, household purchase cadence, frying/cooking preferences, brand switching, retailer margin and neighborhood-boutique economics. Supermarket assortment cannot proxy Senegal.

## 9. Sesame: export crop versus processing candidate

2024 WITS reports 27.066m kg sesame-seed exports worth USD 20.052m, heavily concentrated in China. ANSD reports only 18,573 t domestic production for 2024/25, creating a timing/stock/methodology/re-export reconciliation question because calendar-year exports exceed the campaign production figure.

ITA says it has characterized sesame varieties and developed a sesame press. Feedipedia shows several distinct value chains: dehulled seed/food meal, roasted sesame oil, paste, mechanically pressed oil and feed meal.

**Decision tree:** raw cleaned/graded export vs hulled seed vs paste/tahini vs premium oil vs ingredient/meal. Compare value added per tonne of scarce seed, buyer specifications, yield, quality, capex, working capital and market depth. Do not divert seed into oil merely because processing sounds more industrial.

## 10. Local fabrication is already real

Evidence:
- SISMAR manufactures groundnut cleaning/sorting equipment for the national program.
- ITA explicitly develops food-processing equipment and lists a sesame press.
- ITA conducts process/product R&D for oil-bearing plants and co-product valorization.

**Next engineering acquisition:** obtain machine functions/specs/BOMs and compare what can be fabricated in Senegal: cleaners, aspirators/tarares, shellers/dehullers, roasters/conditioners, screw presses, settling/filtration, tanks, conveyors, cake breakers, dryers, bagging/filling. Identify irreducible motors, bearings, food-grade controls, precision components and testing equipment rather than labeling whole machines 'imported'.

## 11. Candidate hypotheses now open — none promoted

A. Collection-point cleaning/sorting + quality/traceability service/equipment.
B. Groundnut procurement/storage/quality platform that raises utilization of existing processors.
C. Whole-groundnut processor with differentiated edible products plus oil/cake/shell economics.
D. Aflatoxin prevention/testing/segregation infrastructure.
E. Feed-ingredient company using safe local oilseed co-products in formulated blends.
F. Sesame cleaning/grading/hulling/ingredient export before oil.
G. Premium sesame oil/paste only if value per tonne beats seed export and market depth is proven.
H. Regional crude-oil refining/packaging strategy for the large palm/vegetable-oil basket.
I. Local oilseed-processing machinery and maintenance company.
J. Shared processing service for smaller processors/cooperatives.

## 12. What we still do not know

- actual installed and usable crush/refining capacity by company/site;
- current utilization by plant and cause decomposition;
- farmgate → collector → processor/exporter margin stack;
- storage losses/moisture/aflatoxin prevalence by region/season;
- actual industrial extraction yields and energy use in Senegal;
- price/specification of safe peanut and sesame meal to feed mills;
- delivered soybean-meal benchmark and formulation economics;
- household oil consumption by oil type/channel/income;
- informal seggal volume and economics;
- refinery economics for imported/regional crude palm vs domestic seed crushing;
- sesame buyer specs and price premiums for hulled/cleaned/organic/traceable product;
- machine BOM/cost/throughput and Senegal fabrication economics.

Until these gaps are filled, no factory recommendation passes the gate.
