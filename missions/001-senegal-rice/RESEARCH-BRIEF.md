# Mission 001 research brief — Senegal rice

**Plan status:** AWAITING FOUNDER APPROVAL  
**Research status:** NOT STARTED  
**Launch authority:** Founder  
**Mission director:** Unassigned  
**Decision order:** system truth → build candidates → investment/factory scenarios

## Purpose

Build a decision-grade, geographically specific understanding of Senegal's rice system that identifies real needs, value/capability gaps, potential ventures and shared infrastructure, and ideas that should not be built. This is an industrial decision mission, not merely an academic sector report and not a mandate to justify a mill or factory.

## Six controlling questions

1. **What is happening?** Map production, consumption, imports, exports, prices, varieties, geography, seasonality, participants, and uncertainty.
2. **Why is it happening?** Test irrigation, water, seed, yield, losses, drying, storage, milling, grading, consistency, cleanliness, sensory performance, finance, logistics, distribution, packaging, trust, convenience, and other evidenced explanations.
3. **Where does money leak?** Quantify when defensible: imports, losses, under-processing, logistics inefficiency, unused material streams, imported machinery/inputs, and other leakage. Distinguish gross flows from realistically contestable value.
4. **What could we build?** Discover rather than assume opportunities across production/services, seed, irrigation, machinery services, drying, storage, milling, quality/testing, packaging/brands, logistics, feed, bran products, bran oil, flour/starch, husk applications, software/data, finance, and unexpected categories.
5. **What shared infrastructure unlocks multiple industries?** Test power/heat, water, storage, logistics, laboratories, machinery, maintenance, finance, data, and other multi-user systems.
6. **What should we not build?** Identify attractive-looking ideas defeated by demand, economics, agronomy, regulation, competition, logistics, scale, infrastructure, execution, or opportunity cost.

## Scope

National Senegal, disaggregated wherever the evidence allows. Geographic units must be named rather than inferred; comparisons must flag boundary changes. Prioritization covers producing basins/regions, processors, consumption centers, ports/import channels, corridors, and relevant neighboring markets. Neighboring markets are included for trade and system interaction, not silently merged into Senegal.

The baseline is the latest reliable evidence available at collection time. Seek comparable annual observations covering at least ten years where possible. Every result carries data period, publication/access date, geography, definition, and source. Older still-relevant sources are historical/contextual, never current by implication.

## Workstreams

1. System baseline and subnational geography.
2. Production, agronomy, suppliers, harvest, and post-harvest.
3. Trade, market structure, finance, prices, logistics, and distribution.
4. Dedicated consumer, product-quality, retail, brand, and packaging research.
5. Complete industrial process and quality/safety/standards chain.
6. Machinery, maintenance, spares, skills, localization, and redesign.
7. Energy, water, storage, logistics, laboratories, finance, and other shared infrastructure.
8. Material-stream and circularity mapping.
9. Independent blinded discovery.
10. Opportunity synthesis, economic scenarios only when eligible, and red team.

## Consumer and packaging workstream

Separate measured product performance from package/brand perception. Do not presume foreign or local preference. Eventually compare local and imported products under consistent protocols for grain consistency, impurities, aroma, cooking behavior, taste, pack durability/quality, convenience, sizes, typography, trust signals, shelf visibility, recognition, price, distribution, and availability. Use blind product tests where safe and suitable, then separately test identified packs/brands. Mission 001 may inform future KHOUDIA, KHOUDIA NDAO, NDAO, or other development but selects no brand winner.

## Industrial engineering and circularity

Trace seed/input → cultivation → harvest → drying → storage → milling → sorting/grading → quality control → packaging → warehousing → distribution. Assess several evidence-appropriate scales, not a default mega-factory. For each equipment function distinguish purchase now, local assembly, local fabrication, and potential local redesign; include power/heat, water, maintenance, spares, operator competence, reliability, and downtime.

Account for every measurable input and output. Use `material stream` or `by-product` until disposition is established; do not label a stream waste before uses are investigated. Test broken rice, bran, husks, and newly observed streams for safe food, feed, energy, material, or industrial pathways, subject to composition, consistency, volume, buyer requirements, regulation, logistics, and economics.

## Non-goals

No predetermined factory, brand, technology, supplier, or investment thesis; no advocacy target; no external publication or investment advice; no outreach before explicit approval; no combined wealth-created metric; and no inference of identity-based ownership.
