# Fieldwork questions generated for the consumer workstream

**Status:** draft question bank; fieldwork and external contact remain unauthorized.

## Design principles

* Recruit and analyze by relevant geography, channel, household/food-service role, use occasion and economic segment rather than treating “the Senegalese consumer” as homogeneous.
* Use participants' own product categories before imposing analyst labels.
* Randomize order where possible and distinguish blind product tests, package-only tests, disclosed-product tests and realistic purchase tasks.
* Do not collect unnecessary personal data. Obtain approved consent before interviews, images, audio or product-use observation.
* Record refusals, incomplete tasks and the available choice set. Do not infer ethnicity, ancestry or identity.

## Screening and context

1. Who decides which rice to buy, who purchases it, and who cooks it?
2. Where is rice usually bought, how often, and what quantities are feasible at one purchase?
3. Which dishes or occasions does the household or food-service operation prepare with rice?
4. Which products were genuinely available at the most recent purchase?
5. What constraints—cash on hand, credit, travel, stock, time or cooking equipment—limited that choice?

## Product quality, asked independently of branding

1. How does the participant describe the grain before cooking, without seeing a brand or origin claim?
2. Which visible defects matter, and can they be measured in the tested sample?
3. How are sorting, washing, soaking, water, fuel, cooking time and holding handled for the named dish?
4. How does the coded sample perform on aroma, texture, taste, consistency, swelling and leftovers/reheating?
5. Would the participant choose the same sample for another dish or use occasion? Why?
6. Are observed differences stable across repeat lots rather than a single pack?

## Price and availability, separated from intrinsic quality

1. What price was actually paid, on what date, at which channel, for what net quantity and payment terms?
2. What other products and pack sizes were available at that time?
3. At realistic price points, which coded product would be purchased? Include a no-purchase/current-choice option.
4. Does choice change when purchase quantity or cash outlay changes while unit price is visible?
5. What happens when the preferred product is unavailable, and what substitute is chosen?

## Packaging and information, separated from food

1. Without tasting, what does each anonymized pack communicate about contents, safety, origin and quality?
2. Which pack features aid carrying, opening, pouring, measuring, closing, storage and protection?
3. What has failed in packs previously: tears, seals, labels, moisture protection, pests or inaccurate quantity?
4. Which label elements are understood, misunderstood or ignored?
5. Does seeing a pack, brand, seller, origin, quality mark or certification change choice relative to the blind product result?
6. Can the participant distinguish confidence in the physical product from confidence created by the package or seller?

## Retail and traditional-market observation

1. Which rice products, specifications, origins, pack sizes and prices are present at the observation time?
2. Which are out of stock, behind the counter, in bulk, repacked, prominently displayed or promoted?
3. What information is visible at point of choice, and what does the seller provide orally?
4. Are price and availability observations repeated sufficiently to distinguish a temporary stock event?
5. How are packages handled, stored and exposed to moisture, heat, contamination or damage?

## Household versus food service

1. What operational attributes matter for batch size, speed, fuel, holding, portion yield and consistency?
2. Who specifies and who pays for the product?
3. How costly are sorting, washing, failed batches and inconsistent lots?
4. Is a change in product acceptable to cooks, owners and diners, and under what conditions?

## Closing and falsification

1. What evidence would make the participant switch away from the current choice?
2. What claimed product benefit is least credible, and why?
3. Is the presumed preference actually explained by price, stock, habit, seller recommendation or cooking risk?
4. What important choice factor did the research team fail to ask about?

## Instrument development still required

Before deployment, translate and cognitively test questions; establish consent and data-protection procedures; define product procurement and food-safety protocols; pre-register the primary comparisons where appropriate; create enumerator guidance; and approve sampling, compensation and analysis. Brand-name testing is outside this question bank until separately approved.
