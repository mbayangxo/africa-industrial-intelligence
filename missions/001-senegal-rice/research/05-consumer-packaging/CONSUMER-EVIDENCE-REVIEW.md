## Analytical framework for the resumed desk review

Evidence must be captured without collapsing different choice mechanisms into a generic idea of “preference.” Each finding will be assigned to one or more of these lanes, and causal language will be used only where the study design supports it.

### Product quality

* Grain type, variety, origin, proportion broken, uniformity, impurities, moisture, age and storage condition.
* Aroma before and after cooking, taste, texture, swelling, cooking time, water absorption, holding/reheating behavior and suitability for a named dish.
* Food-safety or conformity measurements, with the tested lot, method and standard identified.
* Whether an attribute is measured instrumentally, observed by a researcher, reported by a participant, or inferred from purchasing behavior.

### Brand and packaging perception

* Recognition and recall of a brand, pack, producer, origin claim, quality mark or certification.
* Trust, perceived cleanliness or quality, prestige, visual appeal and interpretation of origin.
* Pack material and integrity, sealing and tamper evidence, label readability, size, carrying, pouring, storage and resealability.
* Blind tests must be distinguished from branded or information treatments. Packaging perceptions must not be reported as product-quality findings.

### Price and affordability

* Observed transaction price versus posted or recalled price.
* Unit-normalized price, pack size, purchase frequency, credit, promotion and household cash-flow constraints.
* Stated willingness to pay versus incentive-compatible choice or actual purchase behavior.
* Original XOF values and date/location must be preserved; any conversion must record its method.

### Availability and distribution

* Presence, stock-outs, assortment, pack sizes and shelf or stall visibility by channel and location.
* Proximity, travel/time cost, delivery, wholesaler and food-service availability.
* Whether a choice reflects preferred attributes or simply the feasible set available to the buyer.

## Required segmentation

The resumed review must test whether findings differ by geography, urban/rural setting, income or expenditure group, household characteristics, purchasing channel, household versus food service, dish/use occasion, and grain type. Findings from one sampled place or population must not be generalized nationally without evidence.

## Evidence appraisal rules

For each study or dataset, record:

* research question, date and geography;
* population, sampling frame, sample selection and analytic sample;
* product definitions and comparison conditions;
* revealed behavior, stated preference, sensory test, auction/choice experiment, observation, interview or administrative data;
* whether products were blind, branded, packaged, priced and equally available;
* original questionnaire or protocol availability;
* statistical uncertainty and potential ordering, hypothetical, social-desirability, enumerator and selection effects;
* funding and potential conflicts;
* limits on transfer across place, time, dish or consumer segment.
