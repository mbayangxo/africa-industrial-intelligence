# Opportunity Genome and Industrial Graph

A paragraph is not enough to represent an industrial opportunity. AII stores an opportunity's economic anatomy: payer/user, problem, demand pattern, inputs, processes, outputs/co-products, capabilities, infrastructure, machinery, labor, distribution, capital/working-capital characteristics, localization path, evidence and kill conditions.

## Why

This permits cross-opportunity questions:
- Which businesses require the same machinery?
- Which require the same lab, warehouse, cold room, dryer, packaging line or distribution route?
- Can a shared service reach better utilization than each company owning underused equipment?
- Does one process output plausibly feed another?
- Which import-dependent component recurs across factories and could justify local manufacturing?
- Which capability appears across sectors and therefore deserves a dedicated mission?
- Which operating company could create demand for a second company?

## Guardrail

Graph adjacency is not economic feasibility. A co-product/input match remains a hypothesis until specification, safety, seasonality, volume, logistics, price and buyer acceptance are evidenced.

Likewise, a shared capability used by five candidate businesses is not automatically the first thing to build. The graph exposes dependencies and potential leverage; build decisions remain evidence- and human-governed.

## Future continental graph

As missions expand, the same structure can connect country-level opportunities without pretending markets are identical. Nodes retain geography, standards, prices, logistics and evidence dates. Cross-border adjacency generates a research question, not an assumption.
